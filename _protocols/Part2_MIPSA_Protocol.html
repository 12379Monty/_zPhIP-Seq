A1  ·  pDEST-MIPSA Vector Design
Low Variability
Description
  • MIPSA Gateway Destination vector (pDEST-MIPSA) encodes in order: T7 promoter, UCI barcode, E. coli RBS, N-terminal HaloTag (891 nt), FLAG epitope, Gateway recombination sites (ORF insertion), I-SceI site
  • UCI barcode sequence: (SW)₁₈–AGGGA–(SW)₁₈ where S = C/G and W = A/T — isothermal design ensures balanced GC content
  • Each ORF inserted by Gateway LR recombination from entry clone library (e.g. human ORFeome)
  • Each ORF represented by ~10 UCIs (unique barcoded copies) — the fundamental redundancy that enables probe-aggregation analysis
QC Measures
  • Sanger sequencing of recombined clones to verify correct ORF orientation and reading frame fusion to HaloTag
  • Deep sequencing of plasmid pool to verify UCI barcode diversity (target: ~10 UCIs per ORF)
  • Confirm no frameshifts or stop codons introduced at recombination junctions
Variability Introduced
  • Vector design is fixed — minimal variability at this step
  • Gateway recombination efficiency varies by ORF size but is well-established
  • UCI diversity is stochastic by design — the isothermal sequence ensures uniqueness and identifiability
  • ~10× UCI redundancy per ORF is the key design feature enabling multi-probe statistical aggregation
A2  ·  Plasmid Linearisation
Low Variability
Description
  • Circular pDEST-MIPSA plasmid pool linearised at the I-SceI restriction site downstream of each ORF
  • Linearisation prevents read-through transcription beyond the ORF, which would produce RNA templates of uncontrolled length
  • Uncontrolled length would interfere with RT-priming and HaloTag cis-coupling in Phase B
  • Linearised DNA purified by column or ethanol precipitation
QC Measures
  • Gel electrophoresis to confirm complete linearisation (no supercoiled band remaining)
  • DNA quantification post-purification (Qubit or NanoDrop)
  • Any residual circular template will produce heterogeneous RNA and must be eliminated before IVT
Variability Introduced
  • I-SceI is a highly efficient and specific homing endonuclease — low intrinsic variability
  • Incomplete linearisation is the primary failure mode and is readily detected by gel electrophoresis
  • Operator error (wrong buffer, wrong temperature) is the main risk

B1  ·  In Vitro Transcription (IVT)
Medium Variability
Description
  • Linearised pDEST-MIPSA pool used as template for in vitro transcription using T7 RNA polymerase
  • Produces pool of RNA molecules, each containing: UCI barcode, HaloTag coding sequence, ORF sequence
  • RNA serves dual role: translation template (encoding HaloTag-ORF fusion protein) AND the molecule to which the UCI cDNA barcode attaches in B2
  • RNA purified and quantified; stored at −80°C in small aliquots to avoid freeze-thaw degradation
QC Measures
  • RNA integrity check (Bioanalyzer or TapeStation) — degraded RNA reduces translation efficiency
  • RNA yield and concentration (Qubit)
  • Confirm expected size distribution of transcripts across ORF sizes
  • RNase contamination check — strict RNase-free technique required throughout
Variability Introduced
  • RNA is inherently less stable than DNA — the most chemically fragile intermediate in the pipeline
  • Transcription efficiency varies by template GC content and secondary structure
  • RNase contamination is a critical risk — a single contaminated reagent can destroy the entire pool
  • RNA degradation between IVT and the coupled RT/translation step is a meaningful source of per-ORF efficiency variability
B2  ·  Reverse Transcription with HaloLigand-Conjugated Primer
Feature Heterogeneity
Description
  • RT primer conjugated to a succinimidyl ester–haloalkane (HaloLigand) moiety annealed to each RNA at position −32 nt upstream of the AUG start codon
  • Position −32 was empirically determined: primers within 20 nt of RBS interfere with ribosome assembly and abolish translation; −32 is the closest position that does not reduce translation yield
  • Reverse transcriptase synthesises cDNA from the UCI barcode region, producing an RNA:cDNA hybrid
  • Resulting structure: HaloLigand-conjugated cDNA physically proximal to the ribosome entry site, poised for cis coupling during translation in B3
QC Measures
  • Confirm RT primer incorporation by gel or bioanalyzer
  • RT efficiency assessed by qPCR on cDNA product
  • Confirm HaloLigand coupling chemistry is intact (supplier QC certificates; functional validation by test translation with FLAG western blot)
  • Primer batch QC — each new lot must be validated before use in library-scale experiments
Variability Introduced
  • Most technically demanding and variable step in the MIPSA protocol
  • RT efficiency varies with RNA secondary structure, template GC content, and primer batch quality
  • HaloLigand conjugation chemistry must be intact — degraded or improperly conjugated primer breaks the barcode-protein link entirely
  • Batch-to-batch variability in the HaloLigand-conjugated primer is a real and important risk
B3  ·  Cell-Free Translation and Cis HaloTag Self-Assembly
Feature Heterogeneity
Description
  • RNA:cDNA hybrid pool added to E. coli cell-free translation system
  • Release factors excluded from reaction to stall ribosomes at stop codons — improves cis-coupling efficiency
  • Translation order: HaloTag (N-terminal) → FLAG → ORF protein
  • As nascent HaloTag folds, it forms an irreversible covalent bond with the HaloLigand moiety on the nearby UCI cDNA — linking barcode to protein in cis
  • ~50% of protein-UCI conjugates formed in cis; ~50% form in trans (a different protein's barcode coupled to a nearby HaloTag)
  • 50% cis efficiency is sufficient for assay performance — trans events contribute to characterisable background noise
QC Measures
  • Anti-FLAG western blot to confirm translation of HaloTag-FLAG-ORF fusion protein
  • Test IP of a known positive antigen (e.g. TRIM21, NT5C1A) with validated positive serum
  • Estimate cis coupling efficiency by pull-down with HaloLigand beads followed by qPCR of UCI region
  • Negative control: translation without HaloLigand-conjugated primer — should produce protein but no protein-UCI conjugate
Variability Introduced
  • Cis vs trans coupling ratio is inherently stochastic and varies by ORF size — larger proteins are released from ribosome before HaloTag matures, favouring trans coupling
  • 50% cis efficiency is an average; actual rate varies per ORF — this is the fundamental source of UCI-level efficiency variation
  • This step is the statistical rationale for a probe-aggregation model: UCI-level efficiency is not uniform and must be estimated, not assumed
  • Release factor exclusion improves cis rate but introduces ribosome stalling — a trade-off managed empirically

C1  ·  Sample Preparation and Dilution
Medium Variability
Description
  • Patient or control serum/plasma diluted in IP buffer (typically 1:100 to 1:500)
  • Processed in 96-well plate format
  • Column 12 reserved for internal controls: serum controls (pooled, consistent across plates) and PBS mock IP controls (beads only, no serum)
  • Serum controls used for inter-plate QC; mock IPs are the primary background reference for data analysis
QC Measures
  • Confirm sample identity and volume
  • Confirm consistent dilution factor across all samples
  • Flag samples with haemolysis or lipaemia (affects IgG recovery)
  • Internal serum controls must be from same pooled lot across all plates in a study
Variability Introduced
  • Sample quality (haemolysis, freeze-thaw history) varies across biobank specimens
  • Dilution factor affects the competition landscape — higher dilution reduces sensitivity but improves specificity
  • Column 12 control design is the most explicit QC structure in the MIPSA operational protocol
C2  ·  Incubation of Serum with MIPSA Library
Medium Variability
Description
  • Diluted serum mixed with MIPSA protein-barcode library and incubated at 4°C with rotation for 16–18 hours
  • Serum antibodies bind to cognate antigens in the library — solution-phase binding, not surface-displayed
  • Solution-phase kinetics more closely approximate native immunoassay conditions than phage display
  • Conformational epitopes accessible because proteins are soluble and folded, not constrained by phage capsid
QC Measures
  • Consistent library input across samples (normalised by protein concentration)
  • Positive control serum with known reactivity included on each plate
  • Mock IP (PBS) and serum-only controls in column 12
  • Temperature and rotation speed logged for each run
Variability Introduced
  • Total IgG varies between individuals — affects competition for library members
  • Temperature fluctuations and rotation inconsistencies affect binding kinetics
  • 16–18 hour window introduces time-of-day variability if plates not processed simultaneously
C3  ·  Immunoprecipitation with Protein A/G Magnetic Beads
High Variability
Description
  • Protein A/G magnetic beads capture IgG-antigen complexes
  • Beads separated magnetically and washed 3–5 times
  • Antibody-bound MIPSA library members (protein + covalently linked UCI cDNA barcode) retained on beads
  • Structurally equivalent to PhIP-Seq C3, but targets soluble protein-DNA conjugates rather than intact phage particles
QC Measures
  • Mock IP control (PBS) run in parallel — primary background reference for all statistical comparisons
  • Positive control antigen recovery confirmed
  • Wash number and volume must be consistent across all samples
  • Wash stringency directly determines the false positive rate
Variability Introduced
  • Same bead-capture variability as PhIP-Seq C3
  • Additional risk: MIPSA proteins are soluble — incomplete washing releases more background material than from phage particles
  • Trans-coupled barcode-protein pairs (from B3) contribute to non-specific background that cannot be distinguished from true signal at the individual library member level
  • Bead lot variation is a batch-level risk requiring inter-lot validation

D1  ·  PCR Amplification of UCI Barcodes
Medium Variability
Description
  • After washing, beads resuspended and UCI cDNA barcodes amplified by PCR
  • Universal primers flanking the UCI region used, with Illumina adapters and sample-specific indices added
  • UCIs are short (~50 bp) — PCR is efficient and requires fewer cycles than for PhIP-Seq inserts
  • Each sample generates one indexed sequencing library
QC Measures
  • Gel electrophoresis to confirm PCR product at expected size
  • Quantify libraries (Qubit or qPCR)
  • Check for primer dimer contamination
  • Confirm index diversity across multiplexed samples
Variability Introduced
  • PCR cycle number affects representation — more cycles introduce greater amplification bias
  • Jackpot effects possible if input barcode counts are very low
  • Short UCI amplicons are less susceptible to length bias than PhIP-Seq inserts
D2  ·  Next-Generation Sequencing
Low Variability
Description
  • Multiplexed libraries pooled and sequenced on Illumina (NextSeq or HiSeq)
  • Target: 1–3 million reads per sample
  • Single-end reads standard — UCIs are short fixed-length sequences
  • Higher alignment rates than PhIP-Seq due to short, structured barcode targets
QC Measures
  • Per-sample aligned read count (target: >500,000)
  • Sequencing quality scores (Q30 threshold)
  • Alignment rate to validated UCI dictionary (target: >90%)
  • Per-sample Pearson correlation with serum controls (target: ≥0.90 among internal controls)
Variability Introduced
  • NGS is the most reproducible step in the pipeline
  • Main risk: uneven sample pooling before sequencing run
  • Run-to-run variability is low given standardised Illumina chemistry

E1  ·  Barcode-to-ORF Mapping and Count Matrix Generation
Low Variability
Description
  • Raw reads aligned using Bowtie2 to a validated dictionary of mono-associated barcode-ORF pairs
  • Only UCIs confirmed to associate with a single ORF (by prior sequencing of unselected library) are retained
  • Count matrix generated: rows = UCI-ORF pairs, columns = samples, values = read counts
  • Multi-UCI structure per ORF is explicit in the matrix — each ORF has ~10 rows (one per UCI)
QC Measures
  • Alignment rate to validated dictionary (target: >90%)
  • Per-sample read depth
  • Pearson correlation heatmap of serum controls across plates (target: ≥0.90)
  • Flag samples with anomalously low read counts or low inter-control correlation
  • PCA — mock IPs must cluster separately from serum samples
Variability Introduced
  • Alignment is a deterministic computational step — minimal variability for fixed aligner parameters
  • Validated UCI dictionary must be version-controlled and matched to the specific library lot used
  • UCI dictionary validation itself is a one-time upstream step with its own sequencing QC
E2  ·  Hit Calling with edgeR
Medium Variability
Description
  • Each sample compared against full set of mock IP controls using edgeR (negative binomial model)
  • Fold-over-background (FOB) calculated per UCI per sample
  • Hits defined at FDR-adjusted p-value < 0.05
  • Secondary confirmation rule applied: antigen called reactive if multiple UCIs targeting same ORF are independently significant
  • Current pipeline treats each UCI as an independent observation — no explicit probe-aggregation model
QC Measures
  • Distribution of FOB values (should be approximately log-normal with heavy right tail)
  • Positive control reactivity confirmed at expected FOB level
  • Mock IPs cluster separately from serum samples in PCA
  • Per-plate Pearson correlation of serum controls ≥ 0.90
Variability Introduced
  • Current per-UCI, per-test approach does not model UCI-level efficiency — the variation introduced in B3 (cis vs trans coupling) is absorbed into noise rather than estimated
  • Multi-UCI confirmation rule is a conservative heuristic — reduces false positives at the cost of sensitivity for antigens with low cis-coupling efficiency or few UCIs
  • FDR-based thresholds carry all the limitations of p-value-derived inference at scale
  • This is the primary analytical limitation motivating the proposed RMA-like probe-aggregation model
