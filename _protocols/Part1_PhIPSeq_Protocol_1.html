A1  ·  Proteome Selection and Peptide Tile Design
Low Variability
Description
  • Protein sequences of interest downloaded in FASTA format (pathogen proteomes, human proteome, allergen panels, or custom)
  • Tiled into overlapping peptide fragments using pepsyn tool with defined length and overlap parameters
  • v1 (2011): 36-aa tiles, 18-aa overlap
  • v2+ (2015 onward): 56-aa tiles, 28-aa overlap — longer tiles improve sensitivity for epitopes near tile boundaries
  • Each peptide back-translated to a DNA sequence optimised for T7 phage expression
  • Flanking cloning sequences appended to each oligo
QC Measures
  • Sequence verification of in silico library against source proteome (completeness check)
  • Confirm even tiling — no gaps in protein sequence coverage
  • Check for duplicate or near-identical tiles that could confound alignment
  • GC content distribution of designed oligos (extreme GC reduces synthesis yield)
  • Reference database version documented and locked
Variability Introduced
  • Library design is entirely computational — variability is minimal if reference proteomes are versioned and pepsyn parameters are fixed
  • Main risk: reference database version drift across studies creates non-comparable libraries
  • Tile length/overlap choice is a systematic design decision, not a source of run-to-run variability
A2  ·  Oligonucleotide Synthesis
High Variability
Description
  • Designed DNA sequences synthesised as a pooled oligonucleotide library on microarray-based platforms (Agilent, Twist Bioscience)
  • Each oligo encodes one peptide tile plus flanking primer sequences
  • Libraries range from 50,000 to >700,000 distinct sequences per synthesis run
  • Synthesised pool eluted and amplified by PCR to generate sufficient material for cloning
QC Measures
  • NGS of amplified oligo pool to verify library complexity (all sequences present) and representation evenness
  • Gel electrophoresis to confirm oligo length distribution
  • Target: each library member within 10-fold of median (in practice, 100-fold range is common)
  • Flag underrepresented sequences before proceeding to cloning
Variability Introduced
  • Microarray synthesis introduces systematic representation bias — some sequences synthesised at much lower yield due to GC content, secondary structure, and positional effects on the array
  • This is a persistent source of library imbalance that downstream normalisation must address
  • One of the most consequential sources of background non-uniformity in the final count matrix
  • Batch-to-batch variation between synthesis runs is real and not fully correctable
A3  ·  Cloning into T7 Phage Display Vector
High Variability
Description
  • Amplified oligo pool cloned into T7 bacteriophage display vector (T7Select system, Novagen/Millipore)
  • T7 preferred over M13: displays larger inserts (up to 1,000 aa), smaller particle (~60 nm), no membrane export requirement
  • Insert fused to T7 10B capsid protein — peptide displayed on phage surface, linking genotype to phenotype
  • Recombinant phage packaged in vitro and used to infect E. coli BLT5403 for amplification
QC Measures
  • Plaque titration to determine phage library titre (target: >10⁸ pfu/ml)
  • PCR of individual plaques to verify insert presence
  • Deep sequencing of packaged library to confirm complexity maintained through cloning
  • Calculate fraction of phage carrying insert vs empty vector
  • Compare representation to pre-cloning oligo pool
Variability Introduced
  • Cloning efficiency is not uniform — sequences encoding toxic or poorly expressed peptides are selectively lost
  • Compounds the synthesis bias from A2: library complexity after cloning is reliably lower than the designed complexity
  • Losses at this step are irreversible — dropped library members cannot be recovered

Library Propagation

B1  ·  E. coli Infection and Phage Amplification
Medium Variability
Description
  • Packaged phage library used to infect E. coli BLT5403 (contains T7 RNA polymerase under IPTG-inducible control)
  • Infected culture grown to lysis; lysate cleared by centrifugation
  • Amplified phage stock generated for use in immunoprecipitation experiments
  • Multiplicity of infection (MOI) — phage:bacteria ratio — must be controlled to ensure uniform amplification
QC Measures
  • Phage titre of amplified stock (pfu/ml)
  • Deep sequencing of amplified stock to verify library complexity maintained vs pre-amplification
  • Confirm no catastrophic dropout of specific library members
  • Monitor lysis time (too fast or too slow indicates culture problems)
  • Working stock stored at 4°C with titration check before each use
Variability Introduced
  • Phage encoding peptides conferring growth disadvantage to E. coli will be underrepresented after amplification
  • Library representation can shift between amplification rounds — particularly for aged or re-amplified stocks
  • Standardising MOI and growth conditions reduces but does not eliminate representation drift

C1  ·  Serum Pre-adsorption
Medium Variability
Description
  • Serum pre-adsorbed against E. coli lysate and/or empty phage particles (no insert) to remove non-specific antibodies
  • Pre-adsorption by incubation at 4°C followed by magnetic bead capture and removal
  • Step introduced to reduce background and improve signal-to-noise ratio
  • Not standardised until the v3 Nature Protocols paper (2018)
QC Measures
  • Confirm reduction in background binding to empty phage controls post-adsorption
  • Monitor serum volume and dilution factor — must be consistent across all samples
  • Pre-adsorption material must be from same lot across a study batch
Variability Introduced
  • Pre-adsorption efficiency varies with serum composition, incubation time, and blocking material quality
  • Insufficient pre-adsorption inflates background — increases false positive rate
  • Excessive pre-adsorption can remove low-affinity true positives
C2  ·  Phage-Antibody Incubation
High Variability
Description
  • Pre-adsorbed serum diluted 1:1000 in PBS-T and incubated with phage library at 4°C with rotation for 16–18 hours
  • Serum antibodies bind to cognate peptide epitopes on phage surface
  • Library used at ~10⁵ phage per library member to ensure adequate representation
  • v3 (2018): input IgG concentration normalised across samples to reduce between-sample variability
QC Measures
  • Confirm consistent phage input across all samples (measured by titre)
  • Confirm consistent serum dilution
  • Positive control sera with known reactivity (e.g. anti-FLAG antibody)
  • Negative controls: PBS or pre-immune serum
  • IgG quantification of input samples if normalisation is applied
Variability Introduced
  • Largest source of sample-to-sample variability in the assay
  • Total IgG concentration varies substantially between individuals — affects competition for library members
  • Temperature fluctuations and rotation speed inconsistencies affect binding kinetics
  • 16–18 hour window introduces time-of-day variability if plates are not processed simultaneously
C3  ·  Immunoprecipitation with Protein A/G Magnetic Beads
High Variability
Description
  • Protein A/G magnetic beads capture IgG-phage complexes from the incubation mixture
  • Beads separated by magnet and washed 3–5× with PBS-T to remove unbound phage
  • Bound phage retained on beads contain the peptides recognised by the individual's antibodies
  • v1 (2011): two rounds of panning performed
  • v3 (2018): single-round panning became standard — two rounds over-enriched non-specific phage and reduced quantitative linearity
QC Measures
  • Positive control recovery confirmed (known reactive serum + cognate phage)
  • Mock IP control (PBS, no serum) run in parallel — primary background reference for data analysis
  • Phage titre in wash fractions to confirm adequate washing
  • Consistent bead volume and wash stringency across all samples
Variability Introduced
  • Bead capture efficiency varies with bead lot, total IgG load, and wash conditions
  • Non-specific sticking of phage to beads is a persistent source of background
  • Switch from 2-round to single-round panning (v2→v3) significantly reduced non-specific enrichment — historical datasets are not directly comparable to modern data without harmonisation

D1  ·  PCR Amplification of Phage Inserts
Medium Variability
Description
  • Phage eluted from beads by boiling after washing
  • Insert DNA amplified by PCR using universal primers flanking the cloning site
  • Illumina sequencing adapters and sample-specific index barcodes added in same or subsequent indexing PCR
  • Converts immunoprecipitated phage pool into Illumina-compatible sequencing library
  • PCR cycles minimised (typically 20–25) to avoid over-amplification artefacts
QC Measures
  • Gel electrophoresis to confirm PCR product at expected size
  • Library quantification by qPCR or Qubit fluorometry
  • Check for primer dimer contamination
  • Confirm index diversity across multiplexed samples
  • Minimum input phage threshold before PCR — low input risks jackpot effects
Variability Introduced
  • PCR amplification biases toward shorter or higher-GC inserts
  • Jackpot effects (clonal amplification of rare phage) can occur if input is too low
  • Consistent input phage numbers across samples partially control this
D2  ·  Next-Generation Sequencing
Low Variability
Description
  • Multiplexed libraries from all samples pooled and sequenced on Illumina (HiSeq or NextSeq)
  • Target: 1–3 million reads per sample
  • Single-end 50-bp reads standard — inserts are short enough that paired-end adds little information
  • Reads demultiplexed by sample index after sequencing run
QC Measures
  • Per-sample read count (target: >500,000 aligned reads; low counts trigger repeat)
  • Sequencing quality scores (Q30 threshold)
  • Duplication rate per sample
  • Fraction of reads aligning to designed library (unaligned reads indicate contamination or artefacts)
  • Library complexity post-sequencing: number of distinct peptides detected above 1 read
Variability Introduced
  • NGS itself is the most reproducible step in the entire pipeline
  • Run-to-run variability is low given standardised platform and chemistry
  • Main technical risk: uneven pooling of samples before sequencing leads to one sample dominating reads at expense of others

E1  ·  Read Alignment and Count Matrix Generation
Low Variability
Description
  • Raw reads trimmed and aligned to the designed peptide library using Bowtie2
  • Count matrix generated: rows = peptide tiles, columns = samples, values = read counts
  • Tiles with zero counts across all samples flagged for investigation
  • Phippery pipeline commonly used for integrated processing (alignment + QC + normalisation)
QC Measures
  • Alignment rate (target: >85% of reads map to library)
  • Per-sample read depth and count distribution
  • Pearson correlation between technical replicates (target: ≥0.95)
  • Pearson correlation of serum controls across plates (target: ≥0.90)
  • PCA of all samples — controls should cluster separately from patient samples
Variability Introduced
  • Alignment is a deterministic computational step — minimal variability for fixed aligner parameters
  • Parameter choices (mismatch tolerance, multi-mapping handling) affect count matrix but are fixed within a study
E2  ·  Normalisation and Hit Calling
Medium Variability
Description
  • Read counts normalised (RPM or edgeR TMM normalisation)
  • Each sample compared against full set of mock IP controls using edgeR (negative binomial) or BEER (Bayesian) or Z-score
  • edgeR: FDR-adjusted p-value < 0.05 threshold
  • Z-score method: Z = (RPM_sample − mean RPM_mock) / SD RPM_mock; threshold typically Z > 3.5
  • BEER: posterior probability of enrichment + fold-change estimate; higher sensitivity for weak reactivities
QC Measures
  • Distribution of fold-over-background values (should be approximately log-normal with heavy right tail)
  • Positive control enrichment confirmed at expected level
  • Mock IPs must cluster separately from serum samples in PCA
  • Comparison of hit lists between methods for sensitivity check
Variability Introduced
  • Choice of statistical method (edgeR vs BEER vs Z-score) affects hit lists — this is systematic variability, not random
  • Particularly consequential for weak or early-stage antibody responses
  • No probe-aggregation across overlapping tiles is performed in current pipelines — tile-level independence assumption is unverified
  • FDR-based thresholds carry all the limitations of p-value-derived inference at scale
